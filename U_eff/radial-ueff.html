Effective radial potential

Ueff(r) = L²/(2 m(r)r²) + (α²/2) m(r)r² with m(r) = 1/(1+r⁴). Both terms depend on r only through m(r)r² = r²/(1+r⁴), which is unchanged by r → 1/r, so Ueff(1/r) = Ueff(r): the two wells hold equal values, though on a linear axis the inner one is squeezed towards the origin.

The parameter panel is drawn in α and L, where the three boundaries are simplest: α = 2L for the bifurcation, αL = E where the level reaches Umin, and L² + α²/4 = E where it reaches the barrier top. All three cross at (α, L) = (√2E, √(E/2)).

The bands are energy ranges: below the top of the barrier at r = 1 the orbit is trapped on one side of it, above it the orbit crosses r = 1. At L = 0 that boundary is exactly α1D²/4, the sub-barrier condition of the one-dimensional problem.

The sign of α1D² is a different statement, about the parameters rather than about the energy, so it carries its own colour: blue above the line α = 2L and pink below it. The white marker in that panel can be dragged, which moves both sliders at once. The Ueff curve itself switches between the two, so the sign is readable without looking at the parameter plane, where the same sign is the side of the red line α = 2L. Green and red never mean anything but 𝑴².

Where the wells are

Writing s = m(r)r² ∈ (0, ½] gives Ueff = L²/2s + α²s/2, minimised at s = L/α. That value is reachable only while 2L ≤ α, that is α1D² ≥ 0, and then r0² = (α − α1D)/2L with the second well at 1/r0. Past the bifurcation the two wells merge into a single minimum at r = 1.
