The lemniscate as a central projection

The space curve Γ(t) = (t³, t, 1+t⁴), its shadows on the planes X = 0 and Z = 0, and the ray from the origin that carries it onto the plane Z = 1, where it traces the lemniscate. Drag the picture to turn it.

Parameter

Point

Legend

Γ(t) = (t³, t, 1+t⁴)
on X = 0: Z = 1 + Y⁴
on Z = 0: X = Y³
on Z = 1: the lemniscate

Surfaces through Γ

All five carry the curve, and X = Y³ meets Z = Y⁴ + 1 in exactly Γ. The umbrella pinches at O, the node of the lemniscate, and its double line runs up the Z axis from there.

Why the ray lands on it

Scaling a point by 1/Z is what the plane Z = 1 sees, and Γ(t)/(1+t⁴) = (t³/(1+t⁴), t/(1+t⁴), 1). Those are the coordinates of the lemniscate, so the whole curve is one projective object read in an affine chart. As t → ±∞ the ray flattens onto the plane Z = 0 and the image returns to the node, which is why the drawn sweep covers only part of the curve.
